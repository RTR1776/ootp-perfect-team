The July diagnosis is closed. In HD451 your defense was dead last of 30 in errors, fielding percentage, range and zone rating, and it was inflating everyone’s ERA. This season you finished 8th in fielding percentage, 10th in errors, 10th in zone rating and 8th in double plays. That problem is solved — don’t spend anything else on it.

What replaced it is a scoring problem, and it is not subtle. You hit 98 home runs against a league average of 154. Only one org hit fewer. Meanwhile you allowed 154 — exactly average — so whatever park you’re in is not the explanation; it would have suppressed both.

The identity didn’t land. The build is extreme K-avoidance, but the team struck out in 19.1% of plate appearances against a league average of 19.2% — dead average. You got the walks (8.9%, 4th) and none of the contact benefit, because half the lineup isn’t actually low-strikeout: Gibson 25.1%, Springer 26.4%, Wood 25.4%, McGraw 25.5%, Hornsby 24.3%. You paid the power price of the K-avoid archetype without collecting the contact.

The split that decided it

Against left-handers you were 24th with a .710 OPS — mediocre. Against right-handers, who throw most of the innings, you were 30th of 31 at .674, with the 30th-ranked home-run rate and a below-average strikeout rate. There is no version of the K-avoid identity that survives being worse than average at both jobs on the side of the platoon that matters most.

The plan

What to change, in order

  1. Fix the lineup card before you spend a point

    Beltran in center every day. Banks at short, Cholowsky to second — league-wide he’s a 3.93 WAR/650 second baseman and a 1.95 shortstop. Bassler catches against right-handers, Campanella against lefties, Gibson third. Bellinger only ever faces a left-hander. Springer, McGraw and Marte come off the roster entirely. This is free.

  2. Give Cliff Lee a rotation spot

    Sabathia, Blue, Lee, Anderson, Paige is a five-man rotation with real stamina, and it takes 100+ innings away from a bullpen that cost you a win. Johnson at 72 stamina belongs in relief or in the shop; Coombs, at 106 stamina and a 6.58 ERA, belongs in the shop.

  3. Cut the negative-WAR relievers

    McDowell (−1.2), Luque (−0.7) and Gossage (−0.6) were 254 innings of below-replacement work, and the first two grade out badly league-wide too. Henke, Nen, Pappas and Orosco are a serviceable core; you need two arms, not five.

  4. Buy power that doesn’t strike out

    One card fixes the identity contradiction: Hank Aaron, the Perfect 102. Across 7,837 plate appearances in this league he ran a .791 OPS with a 3.9% home-run rate and a 17.5% strikeout rate — a full point of contact better than league average with 60% more power than anyone in your lineup. He plays right field or first base, which are two of your three holes.

  5. Fund it from the dead weight

    McGraw, Connor, Orosco, Coombs, Gossage, Bellinger, McDowell and Luque carry about 681,000 points at last-10 prices. Aaron last traded at 378,000. The moves pay for themselves with change for a corner outfielder.
