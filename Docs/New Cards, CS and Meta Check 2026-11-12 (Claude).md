## The meta-card check (question 4)

**How the engine reads a card:**
- With no play in the event's own series, a card is its calibrated ratings, nothing else.
- With play, the ratings are blended with that play. Each day's play is read against that day's field, weighted by 0.5^(age/30 days), at K = 1,300 PA (bats) or 900 BF (arms).
- As stronger cards arrive, an old card's runs above the field fall on their own, and a card stops being used, its play fades toward its ratings.

**Test:** predict each base card's runs above the field over a coming 14-day window, using only what was known before it. Base copies only, own series only, 4 windows, about 4M PA and 4M BF in each window.
- Residual = actual − predicted, in runs per 700 PA or BF.
- Negative means the card did worse than the engine said.

1. **New cards with no play are not under-rated.** On average they came in about 1 run below their ratings. Among the best-projected new releases:
   - Bats projected +21.4 came in at +18.3.
   - Arms projected +16.0 came in at +13.6.
   - So read a new card's projection at about 85% at the top end.
2. **Old meta cards' edges are real.**
   - Cards that beat their ratings by 10+ kept doing so: the ratings alone would have under-called them by 2 to 6 runs.
   - The blend holds those edges slightly too long, by 0.5 to 2.4 runs in the last three windows. September's −7 came from the thin early by-day data.
3. **James McDonald is not a case of the engine clinging to old data.**
   - His ratings alone project +10 to +11 in the Iron events. His play there in the last 14 days was +8.5 to +13.7, and his play-minus-ratings gap is about 0.
   - He lasted because nothing better came out at Iron. Keuchel, with no play, now ranks #2 of all Iron SPs, ahead of McDonald's base card:
     - Iron Lunch: Pfeffer +1.5, Keuchel −0.7, McDonald −2.7.

4. **Tuning:** a grid over half-life (14 to 45 days, none) and K (0.75× to 3×), on forward error, is flat.
