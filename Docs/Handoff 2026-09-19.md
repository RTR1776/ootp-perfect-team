## The finding worth acting on

`pnpm era:slopes` regresses a card's observed wOBA deviation from its own
series mean against the ratings on its face, per era band, PA-weighted —
6,666 card-series rows over 10.9M PA. **The rating→outcome relationship is
not one curve with an era term on top.**

Runs per 700 PA per +10 rating:

| era | Power | BABIP |
|---|---|---|
| Deadball ≤1920 | **0.17** | **2.88** |
| Free Agency 1977-93 | 1.78 | 2.66 |
| Steroid 1994-2009 | **1.87** | **0.53** |
| Modern 2010+ | 1.67 | 1.16 |

In deadball Power is statistically indistinguishable from zero
(0.30 ± 0.22 wOBA points) while BABIP is 5.14 ± 0.29 — seventeen times the
value. In the steroid era it inverts. Not a range artifact: Power SD is
32-34 in every band.

Against the shipped curves (the printed sensitivity line is PRE-calibration,
so ×0.5114 to compare): at 1977-93 the model prices Power 1.99 vs 1.78
observed — fine — but **BABIP 1.07 vs 2.66 observed**, under-priced ~2.5×,
and worse the further back you go. Gap is under-priced too; Eye is
over-priced nearly everywhere and is zero in modern.

**Caveat before anyone refits on this:** those standard errors are
card-level and therefore optimistic. Cards inside one series share a park, a
field and an opponent pool, so the honest unit is the series, and some bands
rest on **four** of them (Deadball 4, Live Ball 3). Effect sizes are far too
large to be noise; the precision is not what the ± suggests. More series per
band is what the upload push buys. Per-era curve refit is the real next
project, not a tweak.

## Traps found today (all now in RUNBOOK.md)

- **A hand-swapped export is not re-imported.** `import:observed` hashes
  content and will pick up a changed file, but only when something runs it.
  A 2-team sliver of `diamondvariety_26` sat live in the DB for an hour
  after the full 121-team export replaced it on disk. Run
  `pnpm import:observed --series <slug>` after any manual swap.
- **Check team counts on a filing push.** An export taken from the wrong
  screen carries only your own matchup: ~50 rows and 2 teams against ~3,000
  and 120+. It imports clean and quietly dilutes the series average. Three
  slipped in today and are quarantined in `Archive/Truncated/`.
- **Never read a card's KIND off its title.** A Snapshot is titled after its
  SET, so a legal field shows cards reading "Baseball Reference", "World
  Baseball Classic" and "Super Utility" that are all `card_type 7`. Use
  `card_type`. Verified: all 247 cards in the run-26 Diamond Variety export
  are type 2, 6 or 7 and nothing else. (An earlier note on that catalogue
  row claiming the rule rotates weekly came from this misreading and has
  been withdrawn.)
- **Two filers at once will stack dialogs.** Launch it once and leave it
  running; the report now self-dismisses after 90s either way.

**The era-slopes finding is real in direction and wrong in size, and the method had a bug.** `era-slopes.py` demeaned wOBA within each series but not the ratings. A Bronze field and a Diamond field both have mean deviation 0 whatever their mean ratings are, so pooling series of different value windows inside one band pulled every slope toward zero, worst in the bands with the most heterogeneous series. Refitting with series fixed effects (ratings demeaned within series too) and series-clustered standard errors:

| era (series) | Power | Eye | Avoid Ks | BABIP | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deadball ≤1920 (4) | 0.89 ± .25 (was 0.16) | 1.09 | 1.02 | 3.66 ± .29 (was 2.83) | 0.96 |
| Live Ball 1921-45 (3) | 2.88 ± 1.3 | 0.92 | 1.02 | 2.51 | 0.34 |
| Integration 1946-60 (6) | 2.84 | 0.96 | 1.18 | 3.24 | 0.83 |
| Expansion 1961-76 (5) | 2.13 | 0.61 | 1.37 | 2.05 | 0.32 |
| Free Agency 1977-93 (8) | 2.38 | 0.72 | 1.21 | 2.77 | 0.81 |
| Steroid 1994-2009 (8) | 2.18 ± 1.0 | 0.67 | 0.48 ± .45 | 1.24 ± .95 (was 0.79) | 0.60 |
| Modern 2010+ (23) | 2.53 ± .3 | 0.58 (was −0.01) | 1.44 | 2.02 ± .2 (was 1.01) | 0.63 |
| all eras (57) | 2.27 | 0.72 | 1.16 | 2.43 | 0.65 |

Runs per 700 PA per +10 rating. Every series fitted alone tells the same story (BABIP 3.2–4.4 in each of the four deadball series against Power 0.3–1.7; BABIP 1.5–2.7 in every modern series). So: deadball Power is small, not zero; the steroid-era "inversion" is a ±0.95 noise band on eight heterogeneous series and is withdrawn; modern Eye is 0.58, not zero. The modern band now includes the six "default RE" dailies the join was dropping (env_year null, notes say default RE → 2010), which is why it has 23 series.

**Against the model's own calibrated line, PA-weighted over each band's environments (the wrapper now prints this):**

| era | Power obs / model | BABIP obs / model | Avoid Ks obs / model | Gap obs / model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deadball | 0.89 / 0.44 | 3.66 / 1.09 | 1.02 / 0.44 | 0.96 / 0.53 |
| Live Ball | 2.88 / 0.97 | 2.51 / 1.22 | 1.02 / 0.39 | 0.34 / 0.56 |
| Integration | 2.84 / 2.04 | 3.24 / 1.00 | 1.18 / 0.85 | 0.83 / 0.43 |
| Free Agency | 2.38 / 1.88 | 2.77 / 1.01 | 1.21 / 1.00 | 0.81 / 0.44 |
| Modern | 2.53 / 2.65 | 2.02 / 0.89 | 1.44 / 1.68 | 0.63 / 0.41 |

Two conclusions, both firmer than the original. (1) **BABIP is under-priced everywhere**, 2.3× in modern play and 3.4× in deadball; Gap about 1.5×. This is not an era story first, it is a component story: the single calibration slope (0.51) is right for Power in modern environments and wrong for BABIP in every environment, because it was fitted on the total. (2) **Before 1960 the model scales everything down too hard**: Power 2.8 observed against 1.0–2.0 modelled in the Live Ball and Integration bands. The era term compresses the rating→runs map more than play does.

**What to do about it, in order.** Not a per-era curve refit first: the thin bands (four and three series) cannot support one, and the biggest miss is present in the 23-series modern band where the curves have the most data. The cheap, well-supported step is a **per-component calibration**: regress the within-series observed deviation on the model's per-rating run contributions (with series fixed effects) and apply one multiplier per component instead of one scalar, for bats and for arms. That fixes BABIP and Gap in every era with the data already in hand, and moves every BABIP-for-Power trade the optimiser makes. A pre-1960 era multiplier on top of that is the second step, and the per-era curve refit waits for the deadball and live-ball bands to have ten series each.
