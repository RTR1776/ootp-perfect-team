What actually predicts winning — 150 teams, ratings vs results

Correlation between a team's usage-weighted rating (standardized within its league) and its league-relative outcome. This is the normalization frame: OOTP recenters everything on the league, so what matters is not your rating — it's your rating minus the league mean.

Hitting rating → team wOBA
EYE is the strongest single lever (r = .46) and holds up in the multivariate fit (+10.2 per SD, vs +7.5 K-avoid, +7.0 POW, +5.7 BABIP). Nothing is worthless — but plate discipline compounds.
Pitching rating → team FIP (|r|, lower FIP)
HR-avoid edges Stuff as the top pitching lever (−10.5 vs −9.9 per SD). pBABIP barely matters once the others are in — DIPS lives inside OOTP, at team level too.
Everything vs total WAR
Two non-rating drivers: usage-weighted card value — creep — correlates r = .50 with WAR, as much as any rating. Staff size correlates ~0. Card quality wins; card count doesn't.

Castroville Mashers, decoded

cwhit's HD453 team, the week before he was promoted to PEL. Each dot is Castroville's percentile among the 30 HD453 teams — right of the midline is above league average.

How the offense works

Lowest strikeout rate in the league (17.3%), 97th-pct team BABIP (.321), 13th-pct home runs — and still the 93rd-pct offense (.338 wOBA). The lineup is Ott and a 102 Vlad as the only power, surrounded by contact monsters: Ozzie Smith (K-avoid 202, POW 35), a 93-value Johnny Bassler (K-avoid 217), Brouthers, Hodapp. He plays sub-100 cards where the contact profile is elite.

How the pitching works

Not a Control staff — CON is 30th pct. It's an HR-suppression staff: Alexander (HRA 141), Clemens (146), Sabathia (139), Welch (142). 11 starters and 17 total arms (both ~87th pct) spread the innings; HR/9 of 0.886 is among the league's lowest. With league K and BB rates compressed, the homer is the run that kills you — he's priced that in.

The twist: his exact mix isn't the only frontier. Miami Tamps won more WAR in the same league with the opposite build (Power 136, K-avoid 119). What the two share: elite pitching by HR-avoid + Stuff, above-average EYE, and newer-than-league cards. The lever everyone at the top pulls is EYE + HR-avoid; K-avoid/BABIP-vs-Power is a style choice on top of it.
Ballparks are not in the league export, so park choice can't be separated from HRA ratings here. His defense reads good-not-elite (ZR +7.3, 70th pct; efficiency ~league). The HR suppression is at least mostly the arms. If you want the park angle, grab the league home-park list and I'll fold it in.

Where it's heading

PEL already runs 53.5% of its usage on 101+ cards; the HD leagues run ~40–47%. The 102 wave has landed — Cy Young SP is on 20 of 150 teams, plus Vlad Guerrero RF and Cannonball Morris SP — and your read that the ceiling ends around 105 means this repeats several more times. A card's edge decays on a ~6-week clock, which is why the shop release date deserves a column in every buy decision.

Churn: among the 28 teams still in the same league six weeks later, the median roster had 62% of its cards replaced. Churn correlates positively with WAR (r = .27) — the good teams are the ones upgrading. Castroville itself wasn't in HD453 in July; he climbed into it.

Variants — double the density at the top

Share of usage on VAR cards, by league. PEL teams lean on variants twice as hard as HD teams — but Castroville won at 8.6%, and variant usage correlates only r = .25 with WAR. Variants are an accelerant, not a requirement.

The clan tags — CG, HotL, GH & co. really are the good teams

42 of 156 teams carry a clan suffix. They average +2.4 WAR over unaffiliated teams (36.0 vs 33.6, p = .04), and 38% land in the top WAR quartile vs 21% of solos. They are playing exactly the levers from the regression: +0.26 SD EYE, +0.34 SD HR-avoid, more variants (10.0% vs 7.9% of usage), newer cards.

Average WAR by clan
GH is the scariest per-team (41.4 avg across 5). HotL is the biggest (17 teams). CG peaks highest — Castroville. When one of these tags is in your bracket, expect an HR-avoid staff and an up-the-middle defense.
Cards clans play far more than solos (roster-penetration gap)
The clan-distinctive core is up-the-middle defense + contact (Ozzie, Gibson, Beltran, Wagner, Cholowsky) and HR-avoid arms (Clemens, Sabathia, Josh Johnson, Andrew Miller). You already own and start Gibson and Cholowsky.

Method & the normalization frame

Normalization, concretely. OOTP maps each rating to outcomes through rate = env_rate × e^α × (rating/50)^β — but in PT leagues the environment recenters on the league itself. So a 125 defense at 2B is only "good" relative to the 2B ratings actually rostered around you; the same card is a plus defender in HD450 and average in PEL. Every percentile in this report is computed against usage-weighted rostered cards — the bar you're actually judged on — not the card pool.
