The preset is decided by one number: the run value of a sacrifice bunt with runners on 1st & 2nd, nobody out — the most favorable bunt spot there is. If the bunt loses runs there, small ball loses runs everywhere, and you want Sabermetric. Two modifiers: environments under 4.05 R/G get bumped one notch toward small ball (in low-scoring games win probability rewards the one-run play more than run expectancy does), and anything over 20% strikeouts is capped at Balanced — a runner on 2nd with two K-prone hitters behind him just dies there.

Why it lands where it does

The AVG factor beats the HR factor, ~3 to 1

A homer-suppressing park reads as a small-ball park and is usually the opposite. Take 1955 in a 1.14 AVG / .84 HR stadium: the HR suppression alone would say bunt more (+0.058), but the AVG boost alone says bunt never (−0.020) and it wins, dragging 4.45 R/G up to 5.19. If AVG is inflated it's a hitter's park that just doesn't pay in homers — outs get more expensive, not less.

Strikeout rate, not the year

Advancing a runner only pays if the next hitter puts the ball in play. At 1927's 5.3% K rate the bunt converts constantly; at the PT default's 22.6% it strands. This is why 1927 and 1928 are a trap — people see "1927" and think deadball, but those are 5.02 R/G seasons, the highest-scoring eras in the set. They earn Moderate Small Ball because nobody strikes out, not because runs are scarce.

Steal break-even barely moves

Across every environment PT uses, the break-even steal rate with nobody out only spans 76.5% (1919) to 81.7% (2019), and parks move it a point or two at most. Era and park change whether you bunt far more than whether you run. A genuinely fast card is worth running anywhere; a marginal one isn't worth running anywhere.

You can't buy a BABIP park

A high-AVG park hands its gain to every card regardless of rating — and the contact rating is β=0.16 / R²=0.26, near noise, with PBABIP worse. So don't chase contact bats. What is buyable is what the park makes expensive to lack: K-avoidance (β=−0.76, R²=0.88) and EYE (β=1.04, R²=0.87), the two tightest fits in the game.

Tier compounds with era

Iron and Bronze events cap card value at 49–69. Since HR ∝ (POW/50)^1.33, weak cards deaden the park on top of the era. An Iron event in 1968 is deader than the 1968 number alone says. Shift low-tier historical events one notch further toward small ball.

Tactician is a roster call

It cranks pinch-hitting, L/R matchups and quick hooks — which pays only when your bench is close to your starters, and that's about your roster, not the era. The one era-independent hook: no-DH events, where the pitcher's spot creates real pinch-hit and double-switch leverage. Those carry a no DH tag in the lookup.

Method & caveats

  • Run expectancy. A 24-state base/out Markov chain solved to convergence, driven by that environment's K / BB / HBP / HR / 2B / 3B / BABIP rates. Includes GIDP (11.5% of groundouts with a man on first and under two outs), sacrifice flies, and era-varying ground-ball share. The same engine runs in this page, so the calculator is not an approximation of the table — it is the table, re-solved.
  • R/G is reported on the stated scale. The raw model runs 2–9% under each era's real R/G because it doesn't credit errors, wild pitches or passed balls. So park-adjusted R/G is the era's stated figure moved by the model's relative response: stated × (model_park / model_neutral). Absolute levels stay honest; the 4.05 threshold means the same thing everywhere.
  • Park factors apply to the era rates before solving: BABIP × AVG, HR × HR, 2B × 2B, 3B × 3B. Walks and strikeouts are unaffected by parks. L/R factors are blended by your stated lineup handedness.
  • The AVG factor is the sensitive input. How much of it lands on balls in play rather than on overall average changes the call — the calculator shows a sensitivity strip for exactly this. When readings disagree, trust the shared conclusion, not the point estimate.
  • Era rates. 18 of the 25 historical years come from the Card Lab's fitted era table (PT Card Lab.html). Seven — 1933, 1942, 1960, 1969, 1979, 1983, 1986 — were derived from MLB Batting Year-by-Year Averages.xls. On overlapping years the two agree to about ±0.005 on K rate.
  • Break-even steal rates run 2–4 points high versus published MLB tables, for the same missing-advancement reason. The ordering across environments is what the recommendation uses, and that's unaffected.
  • Tournaments and their parks rotate during the year. The lookup list came from a 2025 scrape of engine/config/tournaments.json and nine events already appear under two different eras. Treat it as a starting point and run the actual stadium through the calculator.
  • Unorthodox (Random) is never the right answer — it's a randomizer, so its expected value is the average of the other presets minus the cost of sometimes being badly wrong.
